Marginal Distributions [DS 3.5] [Lec 10]

Often, we start with a joint distribution of two RVs and we then want to find the distribution of just one of them.

The distribution of one random variable \(X\) computed from a joint distribution is also called the marginal distribution of \(X\). Each random variable will have a marginal cdf as well as a marginal pdf or pmf.

First, how do we learn about the distribution of \(X\) from the joint cdf \(F(x,y)\)? The marginal cdf of \(X\) is \[F_X(x) = \lim\nolimits_{y \to \infty}F(x,y).\]

1. If \((X,Y)\) is continuous, the marginal pdf of \(X\) is \[f_X(x) = \frac{d}{dx}F_X(x) = \int^\infty_{-\infty}f(x,y)dy;\]

2. If \((X,Y)\) is discrete, marginal pmf of \(X\) is \[f_X(x) =\sum\nolimits_{y} f(x,y).\]

3. If \((X,Y)\) follow a mixed distribution (with \(X\) discrete and \(Y\) continuous) with joint pmf/pdf \(f\), then the marginal pmf \(f_X\) of \(X\) can be obtained by:

\[f_X(x) = \int_y f(x,y) \text{\; for all\;} x.\]

and the marginal pdf \(f_Y\) of \(Y\) can be obtained by: \[f_Y(y) = \sum_x f(x,y) \text{\; for \;}-\infty < y < \infty.\]

TipExample

Recall the example of the joint pmf \(f\) of the number of cars \(X\) and the number of television sets \(Y\) for each randomly selected household. The joint pmf is as follows (each entry is \(f(x,y)\)):

\(Y\)
2 - 5 \(X\) 1 2 3 4
1 0.1 0 0.1 0
2 0.3 0 0.1 0.2
3 0 0.2 0 0

What is the marginal distribution of \(X\)? Of \(Y\)?

TipExample

Take the joint cdf \(F(x,y)\) that takes values \(\frac{1}{16} xy(x+y)\) for \(x\in [0,2]\) and \(y\in [0,2]\). Find the marginal cdf \(F_X(x)\) (hint: don’t just plug in \(y=\infty\)) and marginal pdf \(f_X(x)\).

Suppose the joint pdf of \((X,Y)\) is \(f(x,y) = c x^2y \cdot \mathbbm{1}_{ x^2 \le y <1}\).

Independent Random Variables [DS 3.6] [Lec 10]

For a pair of events \(A\) and \(B\), they are independent if and only if \[P(A\cap B) =P(A) P(B).\] We will use the independence of events to define the independence of random variables.

NoteDefinition

two random variables are independent.

Two random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, denoted as \(X \perp Y\), if \[\mathbb{P}(X\in A, Y\in B) = \mathbb{P}(X\in A) \mathbb{P}(Y\in B)\] holds for all \(A\) and \(B\).

Let \(A=(-\infty,x]\) and \(B=(-\infty,y]\), then by definition, \[F(x,y) = F_X(x)F_Y(y).\] Since this holds for any \(x\) and \(y\), the implication is: the independence of \(X\) and \(Y\) implies the factorization of joint cdf into the product of the marginal cdf’s. In fact, the reverse direction also holds.

ImportantTheorem

Factorization of joint cdf.

\(X \perp Y\) if and only if for all \(x,y \in \R\), \[F(x,y) = F_X(x)F_Y(y)\]

Next, the product of pdfs (or pmfs) must also equal the joint pdf or pmf (or pdf/pmf) for independent random variables.

ImportantTheorem

Factorization.

If \(X\) and \(Y\) have joint pdf, joint pmf, or joint pmf/pdf \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)\), then \[X \perp Y \Leftrightarrow f_{X,Y}(x,y) = f_X(x)f_Y(y), \forall x,y \text{ in their supports}\]

Let us see this in an example.

TipExample

A carnival game consists of rolling a fair die, tossing a fair coin two times, and recording both outcomes. Let \(Y\) stand for the number on the die, and let \(X\) stand for the number of heads in the two tosses.

  • Are random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) independent?

  • What is the marginal distribution of \(X\)? Of \(Y?\)

  • Populate the following table of probabilities

The factorization theorem makes it easier to recognize independence from \(f\). If you find that \(f\) partitions into two parts – one with only \(x\) in it, and another with only \(y\) – then the two random variables are independent.

ImportantTheorem

Factorization.

If \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)\) satisfies \[f_{X,Y}(x,y) =h(x)g(y)\] for all \(x,y \in \bR\), where \(h(\cdot)\) and \(g(\cdot)\) are two univariate functions, then \(X\perp Y\).

(Note, [thm:ind-pdf] is a special case of this, by letting \(h_X(x)=f_X(x)\) and \(h_Y(y) = f_Y(y)\), so that \(f(x,y) = f_X(x) f_Y(y).\))

TipExample

Examine the following joint pdfs \(f\) and determine if \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent.

  1. (a) \(f(x,y) = cx^2y^2 \cdot \mathbbm{1}_{x^2+y^2\le 1}.\)

  2. (b) \(f(x,y) = c \mathbbm{1}_{x^2 \le 1, y^2\le 1}.\)

  3. (c) \(f(x,y) = c e^{ - x-2y} \cdot \mathbbm{1}_{x\ge 0, y\ge 0}.\)

(Hint: When the support of \((X,Y)\) have boundaries that are curved or not parallel to the coordinate axes, you cannot factorize \(f(x,y)\). All independent RVs have rectangular (even if unbounded) support!)

Lastly, any functions (or transformations) of independent random variables are also independent.

ImportantTheorem

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, then \(g(X)\) and \(h(Y)\) are independent for any function \(g\) and \(h\).

TipExample

Prove that constant \(c\) is independent with any random variables.

Conditional Distributions [DS 3.6] [Lec 10]

Recall conditional probability \[P(A|B) =\frac{P(A\cap B) }{P(B)}.\] We generalize the concept of conditional probability to conditional distributions. Recall that distributions are just collections of probabilities of events determined by random variables. Conditional distributions will be the probabilities of events determined by some random variables conditional on events determined by other random variables.

Let us start by defining a conditional probability in terms of discrete random variables.

NoteDefinition

Conditional pmf.

The conditional pmf is \[f_{X|Y}(x|y) = \mathbb{P}(X=x|Y=y) = \frac{f_{X,Y}(x,y)}{f_Y(y)},\] provided \(f_Y(y)>0\).

A conditional pmf \(f_{X|Y}(x | y)\) is a valid probability mass function of \(x\). Also, the conditional probability of an event \(X\in E\) can be calculated as: \[P(X\in E|Y=y) = \sum_{x\in E}f_{X|Y}(x|y) .\]

(We can check for ourselves in the next example!)

TipExample

Auto insurance.

Suppose we have the following joint pmf:

Stolen Brand 1 Brand 2 Brand 3 Brand 4 Brand 5 Total
No 0.129 0.298 0.161 0.280 0.108 0.976
Yes 0.010 0.010 0.001 0.002 0.001 0.024
Total 0.139 0.308 0.162 0.282 0.109 1.000

With the joint pmf, we can answer the following questions.

  • What is the probability of a car being stolen?

  • What is the probability of a Brand 3 car being stolen?

  • Given a car is stolen, what is the probability of the stolen car is Brand \(k\)?

Next, we introduce conditional probabilities of continuous random variables.

NoteDefinition

Conditional pdf.

The conditional pdf is \[f_{X|Y}(x|y) = \frac{f_{X,Y}(x,y)}{f_Y(y)},\] if \(f\) is the joint pdf provided \(f_Y(y)>0\).

Using this, conditional probability of \(X \in E\) for any set \(E\) can be computed as:

\[\mathbb{P}(X\in E|Y=y) = \int _{E} f_{X|Y}(x|y) dx.\]

That is, for each fixed value of \(Y=y\), \(f_{X|Y}(x|y)\) is a valid pdf in \(x\). 1

TipExample

Suppose the joint pdf of \((X,Y)\) is \(f(x,y) = e^{-x} \times 1(0\le y \le x <\infty)\). Find \(f_{X|Y}(x|y)\).

Bivariate Normal distribution.

TipExample

Suppose \(X\) is a randomly chosen point between \(0\) and \(1\), and conditional on \(X=x\), \(Y\) is a chosen as a random value between \(x\) and \(1\). (We can write this succinctly as \(Y|X=x \sim \text{Unif}[x,1]\)).

  • (a) Find the joint pdf \(f(x,y)\).

  • (b) Find the marginal distribution of \(Y\).

TipExample

Suppose \((X,Y)\) is uniformly distributed on the semicircle \[\{(x,y): x^2+y^2 \le 1, x \ge 0\}.\] Find \(f_{X|Y}(x|y)\). In particular, what is \(f_{X|Y}(x|0)\)?

Conditional pmf or pdf from Mixed Distributions.

Let \(X\) be discrete and let \(Y\) be continuous with joint pmf/pdf \(f\). Then the conditional pmf of \(X\) given \(Y= y\) is defined by [eq:condit-pmf], and the conditional pdf of \(Y\) given \(X= x\) is defined by [eq:condit-pdf]. 2

TipExample

Suppose that a machine produces defective and nondefective parts, but we do not know what defection rate (the proportion of defective parts). Let \(P\) stand for the unknown proportion of defective parts among all possible parts produced by the machine. (If we were to learn that \(P=p\), we might be willing to say that the parts were independent of each other and each had probability \(p\) of being defective.) Let us say that if \(n\) items will be examined, \(X\) is the number of items that would be defective.

  • (a) What is the conditional pdf of \(X\) given \(P=p\)?

  • (b) What is the joint pmf/pdf of \(X\) and \(P\)?

  • (c) What is the conditional pdf of \(P\) given \(X=x\)?

For conditional distributions, similar conclusions to those of conditional probability also hold.

Let’s say that \(X\) and \(Y\) are random variables such that \(X\) has pmf or pdf \(f_X\), and \(Y\) has pmf or pdf \(f_Y\). Then, the joint pdf or joint pmf (or if it is a mixed scenario, a pmf/pdf) \(f(\cdot, \cdot)\) is defined as follows:

ImportantTheorem

Multiplication rule.

\[f_{(X,Y)}(x,y)=f_{X|Y}(x|y)f_Y(y) = f_{Y|X}(y|x)f_X(x).\]

TipExample

Suppose \(X, Y\) are independent random variables with distribution \(\text{Unif}(0,1)\). Let \(Z=\lfloor{X/Y}\rfloor\) be the floor of the ratio \(X/Y\). Show the probability \[\mathbb{P}(Z \text{ is an odd number}) = \log(2)/2.\] (Hint: use the alternating harmonic series \(\sum\nolimits^\infty_{k=1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} = \log(2).\))

Next, we learn about the Law of total probability and the Bayes theorem, but for random variables \(X\) and \(Y\).

ImportantTheorem

Law of total probability.

Suppose \(Y\) is continuous, \[f_X(x) = \int f_{(X,Y)}(x,y) dy = \int f_{X|Y}(x|y)f_Y(y) dy .\] Suppose \(Y\) is discrete, \[f_X(x) = \sum_y f_{(X,Y)}(x,y) = \sum_y f_{X|Y}(x|y)f_Y(y) .\]

Using this, we revisit the Bayes theorem.

ImportantTheorem

Bayes theorem for random variables.

\[f_{X|Y}(x,y) = \frac{f_{Y|X}(y|x) f_X(x) }{f_Y(y)}.\]

TipExample

Poisson.

Suppose \(X \sim \exp(\lambda)\) for some \(\lambda >0\), and given \(X=x\), \(Y\sim \text{Pois}(x)\). Find the conditional density of \(X|Y=y\) for some \(y\in \mathbb{Z}_+\).

TipExample

Suppose again \(X\) is a randomly chosen point between \(0\) and \(1\), and conditional on \(X=x\), \(Y\) is a chosen as a random value between \(x\) and \(1\). (We can write this succinctly as \(Y|X=x \sim \text{Unif}[x,1]\)). Find the conditional pdf \(g(x | y)\) of \(X\) given \(Y=y\).

(Hint: Plug in \(y=0.1\) and \(y=0.9\) for a sanity check.)

Lastly, we will learn if \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent the conditional distribution (the pdf or pmf) of \(X|Y\) is the same as the marginal distribution (again, the pdf or pmf) of \(X\).

ImportantTheorem

\(X \perp Y\) \(\iff\) \(f_{X|Y}(x|y) = f_{X}(x)\) for all \((x,y)\) with \(f_{Y}(y) >0\).
