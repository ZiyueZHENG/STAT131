Covariance and Correlation [DS 4.6] [Lec 14]

When we are interested in the joint distribution of two random variables, it is useful to have a summary of how much the two random variables depend on each other. Covariance and correlation attempt to measure that dependence, (note, they only capture a particular type of dependence, namely linear dependence.)

NoteDefinition

Covariance.

For bivariate random variable \((X,Y)\), suppose \(\mathbb{E}[X] = \mu_X\) and \(\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mu_Y\), \[\cov(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)],\] provided \(\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\) exists.

With the above definition, suppose the \(\sigma_X^2, \sigma_Y^2 <\infty\), then: \[\cov(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}[XY] - \mathbb{E}[X] \mathbb{E}[Y].\]

For two random variables, covariance is a numerical summary of their relationship – about how do they vary together (linearly).

TipExample

Suppose the joint pdf of \((X,Y)\) is \[f_{X,Y}(x,y) = \left(2xy+\frac{1}{2}\right) \cdot 1_{x\in[0,1], y\in [0,1]}.\] Let us say we also know that \(\mathbb{E}[X] = \mathbb{E}[Y] = \frac{4}{3}\). (Make sure to try this on your own.)

  • Compute \(\mathbb{E}[XY] = \frac{25}{72}\).

  • Compute \(\cov(X,Y) = \frac{1}{144}\).

Here are some properties of covariance:

  1. Covariance can be negative, while variance cannot be negative.

  2. \(\cov(X,X)=V[X]\);

  3. If \(X\perp Y\), \(\cov(X,Y) = 0\).

  4. Even if \(\cov(X,Y) = 0\), \(X\) and \(Y\) can still be dependent.

TipExample

Zero covariance does not imply independence.

Take an random variable \(X\) that is equally likely to be -1, 0, or 11, as well as \(Y=X^2\). Calculate the covariance of \(X\) and \(Y\).

Zero covariance does imply independence.

Let us next examine the variance of linear sums of random variables. We can write that: \[V[X+Y] = V[X]+V[Y] +2 \cov(X,Y),\] More generally, \[V[aX+bY+c] =a^2 V[X]+ b^2V[Y] +2ab \times \cov(X,Y).\]

TipExample

What is \(V[X-Y]\)? What about when \(X \perp Y\)?

ImportantTheorem

Suppose \(V[X_i]<\infty\) for \(i=1,..,n\), \[V[ \sum \nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i] = \sum \nolimits_{i=1}^n V[X_i] + 2 \sum \sum \nolimits_{i<j} \cov(X_i,X_j).\] If \(\{X_i\}_{i=1}^n\) are independent, \(V[ \sum \nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i] = \sum \nolimits_{i=1}^n V[X_i]\).

A key drawback of covariance is that it is not unit free. If you multiply \(X\) and \(Y\) by constants \(a\) and \(b\), we see that covariance grows by that amount: \[\cov(aX,bY) = ab \cov(X,Y).\]

So it is generally impossible to compare dependence across pairs of random variables based on covariances.

An easy fix is to rescale the covariance so that the new metric is comparable.

NoteDefinition

Correlation.

\[\corr(X,Y) = \frac{\cov(X,Y)}{ \sqrt{V[X]} \sqrt{V[Y]}},\] Alternatively, \(\rho(X,Y) = \frac{\cov(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}\)

ImportantTheorem

Size of correlation.

\(\rho(X,Y) \in [-1,1]\).

TipExample

Suppose \(X,Y\) have joint pdf \(f(x,y) = (x+y) \times 1(x\in [0,1],y\in [0,1])\).

  • Compute \(\cov(X,Y)\).

  • Compute \(\corr(X,Y)\).

Conditional Expectations [DS 4.7] [Lec 14]

Recall expectation is a good one-number summary of a random variable. However, often you want a summary that takes into account additional information about another random phenomenon.

How to formally take the additional information into account? Conditional expectation is the tool of our choice.

NoteDefinition

Conditional Expectation (Mean).

Suppose we have random vector \((X,Y)\) such that \(\mathbb{E}[|Y|] < \infty\). A conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x]\) of \(Y\) given \(X=x\) is defined as follows:

  • If \(Y|X=x\) is continuous, \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x] = \int y f_{Y|X}(y|x)dy\)

  • If \(Y|X=x\) is discrete, \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x] = \sum y f_{Y|X}(y|x)\)

The conditioning information in the definition is \(X=x\), fixed as a specific value.

Generally, if you take \(X\) to be a random variable rather than a fixed value \(X=x\), \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\) is a random variable and a function of \(X\).

Intuitively, we should think of \(X=x\) as representing information that has been revealed to us. \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x]\) is our best guess of the value of \(Y\) given the information we have.

The “other” random variable \(X\) may or may not be related to \(Y\). In fact, it’s interesting to discuss the two extremes:

  1. If \(X \perp Y\), the expectation \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X] = \mathbb{E}[Y]\); it is unchanged even after observing \(X\).

  2. If \(X=Y\), \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X] = X\); if we know \(X\), our best guess is \(X\) itself.

TipExample

The following table is the joint pmf of \((X,Y)\) with \(X\) being household size and \(Y\) being the number of cars owned by the household.

TipExample

Suppose the joint pdf of \(X,Y\) is \(f_{X,Y}(x,y) = (x+y)\cdot 1_{(x,y)\in [0,1]^2}\).

  • Find the conditional density \(f_{Y|X}(y|x)\) for \((x,y) \in \bR^2\).

  • Compute \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\). (hint: this is a random variable.)

  • Compute \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=\frac{1}{2}]\).

Note: Conditional expectation is also an expectation. Therefore, the properties of expectations – functions of random variables (Theorem [thm:lotus]), and linearity (Theorem [thm:linearity]) – also hold.

ImportantTheorem

Law of Iterated Expectation (LIE).

\[\mathbb{E} [\mathbb{E}[Y|X]] = \mathbb{E}[Y].\]

Proof. Proof. This is the “expectation” version of the Law of Total Probability \[\mathbb{E}[Y] = \int yf_Y(y) dy = \int\int yf(x,y)dx dy = \int \left[\int yf_{Y|X}(y|x)dy \right] f_{X}(x) dx\] whereas \(f_{Y}(y) =\int f(x,y)dx= \int f_{Y|X}(y|x) f_{X}(x) dx.\) ◻

TipExample

Suppose \(X\sim \text{Unif}(0,1)\), and \(Y|X=x \sim \text{Unif}(x,1)\). Compute \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\) in the following two ways.

  • First compute the marginal probability density \(f_Y(y)\) of \(Y\). Then, use this to calculate the expectation \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\).

  • Use the law of iterated expectation, i.e., \(\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y | X]]\).

Now, suppose we have a random variable \(Z = h(X,Y)\) that is a function of both random variables \(X\) and \(Y\). Then, \(\mathbb{E}[Z|X=x]\) can be calculated either as

  1. Taking the conditional distribution of \(Z=h(X,Y)\), and calculating the conditional distribution of \(Z\) given \(X=x\), or

  2. Taking the conditional distribution of \(r(x, Y)\) given \(X=x\).

This is formalized in the next lemma.

ImportantLemma

Conditioning is knowing.

Suppose \(Z=h(X,Y)\). Then, the conditional expectation of \(Z\) given \(X=x\) is: \[\mathbb{E}[Z|X=x] =\mathbb{E}[h(X,Y)|X=x] = \mathbb{E}[h({x},Y)|X=x].\] Further, if \(Z\) is continuous, \[\mathbb{E}[Z|X=x] = \int h(x,y)f_{Y|X}(y|x)dy\]

TipExample

Suppose \(X\sim f_X(x)\), and that \(Y=X^2\). Compute \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\) and \(\mathbb{E}[X|Y]\).

Knowing this, we can conveniently define an alternative form for a conditional expectation. First, define \(Z=h(X,Y)=Y\). Then, use the previous lemma to get: \[\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x] =\int y f_{Y|X}(y|x) dy.\] Then, use \(f_{Y|X}(y|x) = \frac{f_{X,Y}(x,y)}{f_X(x)}\).

ImportantLemma

Alternative expression for conditional expectation.

Another formula for conditional expectation is: \[\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x] = \frac{\int y f_{X,Y}(x,y) dy}{f_X(x)}.\]

We are now used to the fact that \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x]\) is a best updated guess for \(Y\) after observing \(X=x\).

There is another important viewpoint to it. It turns out that, when you want to predict \(Y\) after having observed some value of \(X\), the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\) is a very good predictor of \(Y\).

ImportantTheorem

The conditional expectation predicts with the lowest mean squared error (MSE). Specifically, for the random vector \((X,Y)\), the prediction \(d(X)\) of \(Y\) such that its mean squared error \[\mathbb{E}[(Y-d(X))^2]\] is minimized with \(d(X)=\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\).

As we will see in our next lecture, the mean squared error of this optimal predictor \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\) is equal to the conditional variance. 2

TipExample

Let \(X\) be the number of patients out of the first 40 in a clinical trial who have been cured. Suppose that \(P\) has the uniform distribution on the interval [0,1] before the trial begins, and suppose that the outcomes of the patients are conditionally independent given \(P=p\).

  • What is a good prediction before the clinical trial starts?

  • What is the MSE-optimal prediction of \(P\) once you’ve observed \(X\)?

This is a simple but profound result. Given one random variable, our best guess (prediction) for another random variable is the conditional mean. We call this MSE-optimal prediction.

Conditional Variance [DS 4.7] [Lec 14]

Recall we learned about the spread of a random variable \(X\) as the variance. Let us call this marginal variance. We will extend this notion to the variability conditional on another random variable \(X\).

NoteDefinition

For the random vector \((X,Y)\), \[V[Y|X=x] =\mathbb{E}[ (Y- \mathbb{E}[Y|X=x])^2|X=x] .\]

As \(X\) is a random variable, \(V[Y|X]\) is also a random variable and a function of \(X\).

TipExample

Take joint pdf \(f_{X,Y}(x,y) = (x+y) \times 1((x,y)\in [0,1]^2)\) of a random vector \((X,Y)\). The conditional expectation of \(Y\) given \(X=x\) is \(\mathbb{E}[Y|X] = \frac{\frac{1}{2} X + \frac{1}{3}}{X + \frac{1}{2}}\) for \(0 \le X \le 1\). What is \(V[Y|X]\)?

Next, we learn about a key relationship between marginal variance and conditional variance.

ImportantTheorem

Law of total variance.

Also known as Eve’s law: \[V[Y] = E[V[Y|X]] +V[E[Y|X]].\]

Often when the value of the random variable \(X=x\) is revealed, there is less uncertainty about the other random variable \(Y\). That is, the conditional variance \(V[Y|X=x]\) of \(Y\) is smaller than the marginal variance \(V[Y]\).

Here is an example of this.

TipExample

Let us revisit the example of a two-step model where (1) \(X\) is uniform between 0 and 1, and (2) given \(X=x\), \(Y\) is uniform between \(x\) and \(1\).

  • Calculate \(V[Y|X=x]\).

  • Calculate \(V[Y]\)

  • Compare the size of the two (in a graph over values \(0 \le X \le 1\)).

But the reduction in variance is not guaranteed. Take random variable \(Z=XY\) for independent random variables \(X \perp Y\), where \(X\) is equally likely to be \(0\) or \(1\), and \(Y\) is Normal with mean zero and variance 1. Then, the marginal variance is:

\[V[Z] = 1/2.\]

and the conditional variance is

\[V[Z|X=x] = V[xY] = x^2 V[Y] = x^2.\]

Clearly, if \(x\) is bigger than \(\sqrt{1/2}\), the conditional variance \(V[Z|X=x]\) is larger than \(X\). Put another way, there is positive probability that the variance actually increases:

\[\mathbb{P}\left( V[Z|X] > V[Z] \right) = \mathbb{P}\left( X^2 > \frac{1}{2} \right) \simeq 0.707 > 0.\]

So, in what sense is the variance reduced when \(X\) is revealed? On average (in plain language), or in expectation:

ImportantTheorem

Conditional Variance vs. Marginal Variance.

\[V[Y] \ge E[V[Y|X]].\]

The proof is simple: examine the law of total variance.
