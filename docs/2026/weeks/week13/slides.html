Lecture 13: Expectations and Variance

Expectation [DS 4.1-4.2] [Lec 13]

Given a random element \(X\), how to give a one-number summary of it?

Recall the concept of an average of some numbers (or weighted averages, weighted by some relative importance). In probability theory, the average of a random variable is called expectation. Expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X]\) captures the center of a random variable.

NoteDefinition

Expectation.

For discrete random variable \(X\), \[\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum \nolimits_{x \in supp(X)} x f(x),\] provided \(\mathbb{E}[|X|] < \infty\).

For continuous random variable \(X\), \[\mathbb{E}[X] = \int\nolimits_{-\infty}^\infty xf(x)dx,\] provided \(\mathbb{E}[|X|] < \infty\).

The discrete version is essentially a weighted average of \(X\), and the weights are the probabilities.

The expectation a random variable is also called the “mean” of that random variable.

TipExample

Find the expectation (mean) of the following random variables.

  • The random variable \(X=c\) with probability 1.

  • Bernoulli random variable with parameter \(p\).

  • Uniform on \([a,b]\).

  • The random variable \(X\) with pdf symmetric about 0.

Note that expectation can be non-existent, as the integral or the summation may not exist.

Cauchy distribution.

TipExample

Suppose the pmf of the random variable \(X\) satisfies \[f(x) \propto \frac{1}{x^2}, \text{ for } x=1, 2,....\] Prove that the expectation of \(X\) does not exist.

When do we know when the expectation of random variable \(X\) exists? One way is to recognize that \(E[X]\) is bounded if \(X\) itself is bounded. To be more specific, if \(P(X\ge a) =1\), then \(\mathbb{E}[X] \ge a\); if \(P(X\le a) =1\), then \(\mathbb{E}[X] \le a\). So clearly, expectation exists for all absolutely bounded random variables.

NoteDefinition

Moments.

For a random variable \(X\), the means of powers \(X^k\) are called moments, which have useful theoretical properties, and some of them are used for additional summaries of a distribution.

(Later, we will learn about the moment generating function which a related tool that aids in deriving distributions of sums of independent random variables and limiting properties of distributions.)

Next, the expectation of a function of the random variable can be computed as follows:

ImportantTheorem

Expected value of function \(h(X)\) of \(X\). Suppose discrete \(X\sim f\), function \(h:\R \to \R\), then \[\mathbb{E} [h(X)] = \sum_{x \in supp(X)} h(x)f(x) ,\] if the sum is finite.

Suppose continuous \(X\sim f\), function \(h:\R \to \R\), then \[\mathbb{E} [h(X)] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty h(x)f(x) dx,\] if the integral is finite.

This theorem is sometimes called the Law of the Unconscious Statistician (LOTUS).

The relation between the probability of an event \(E\) and expectation is characterized as follows. \[\mathbb{P}(E) = \mathbb{E}[ 1_E] ,\] where \(1_E\) denotes the indicator function of \(E\).

Folded Normal.

TipExample

The square root rule.

Suppose a randomly chosen student’s exm score \(X\) follows Unif\([0,100]\). The instructor applies a transformation to the scores to curve them, using: \[Y=h(X) = 10\times \sqrt{X}.\] For a few concrete examples are: \(h(0)=0\), \(h(100)=100\), \(h(36) =60\), \(h(64)=80, \cdots .\)

  • Find the pdf of \(Y\), and compare it with \(f_X\).

  • Compare the expected score: \(EY-EX>0\)?

  • Compare the proportion of Fails.

ImportantTheorem

Expectation of function of multiple RVs.

Suppose \(Y=h(X_1,X_2,...,X_n)\), and continuous \({\bf{X}} \sim f\), \[\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E} [h({\bf{X}})] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty ... \int_{-\infty}^\infty h(\mathbf{x})f(\mathbf{x}) d\mathbf{x},\] if the integral is finite.

Suppose \(Y=h(X_1,X_2,...,X_n)\), and discrete \({\bf{X}} \sim f\), \[\mathbb{E}[Y] =\mathbb{E} [h(\mathbf{X})] = \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in supp(\mathbf{X})} h(\mathbf{x})f(\mathbf{x}) ,\] if the sum is finite.

TipExample

Suppose a point \((X,Y)\) follows the uniform distribution on the square \([0,1]^2\). We hope to find the expected area of the rectangle determined by \((X,Y)\). That is to find \(\mathbb{E}[XY]\) .

TipExample

Suppose \(X,Y\distras{iid} Exp(\lambda)\), whose pdf is \(f(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}\) and let \[Z= \begin{cases} 3X+1 & \text{ if } X\ge Y \\ 6Y & \text{if } X< Y. \end{cases}\] Find \(\mathbb{E}[Z]\).

Next, we describe two properties of expectations.

ImportantTheorem

Linearity. Expectation of a linear function of \(X\): \[\mathbb{E}[aX+b] = a \mathbb{E}[X] +b.\] Expectation of a linear function of \(\mathbf{X}\): \[\mathbb{E}[ \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n a_i X_i+b] = \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n a_i \mathbb{E}[X_i] +b\]

Linearity is a fundamental property of expectation, and it is a consequence of the integral operation.

TipExample

Find the expectation of Binomial random variable \(X\) with parameters \((n,p)\).

TipExample

Suppose a librarian randomly stacks \(k\) different books on the shelf with the labels of their titles. What is the expected number of books under the correct labels?

Next, when we have independent random variables

ImportantTheorem

Let \(X_1,...X_n\) be independent random variables. Then \[\mathbb{E}[\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i ] = \prod\nolimits_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[ X_i ]\]

Pause and think about the two theorems. They are quite different. The mean of the product of random variables is only the product of means when they are independent. However, the sum of random variables has an expectation regardless of independence.

TipExample

Suppose \((X,Y) \distras{iid} N(0,1)\), The distribution of \(X/Y\) is a Cauchy distribution, whose probability density is \(f(z) = 1/(\pi (1+z^2))\) for \(-\infty < z < \infty\). Find \(\mathbb{E}[X/Y]\).

The product property will be useful when we study moment generating functions.

TipExample

Suppose \((X,Y) \distras{iid} N(0,1)\), find \(\mathbb{E}[e^{aX+bY}]\).

We end by discussing a salient feature of the expected value.

ImportantTheorem

The expectation or mean \(E[X]\) predicts \(X\) with the lowest mean squared error (MSE).

Specifically, if you had to pick one value to predict the (inherently) random variable \(X\), you should pick the mean, since it is the value that incurs the smallest error. Let’s do short derivation; we seek a prediction \(d\) of \(Y\) such that its mean squared error \[\text{M.S.E} = \mathbb{E}[(Y-d)^2].\] Expand this: \[\text{M.S.E} = \mathbb{E}[(Y-d)^2] = \mathbb{E}[ Y^2 - 2d Y + d^2] = (d - \mathbb{E}[Y])^2 - [\mathbb{E}[Y]]^2 + \mathbb{E}[Y^2],\] and notice that, since this is a quadratic equation in \(d\), the choice of \(d\) that minimizes this is \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\).

Variance [DS 4.3] [Lec 13]

In reality, the center of a distribution is not informative enough about the random quantity. We are interested in the how spread out the random variable is. Variance captures the variability or spread of a random variable.

NoteDefinition

Variance.

Variance is defined as \[V[X] = \mathbb{E} [(X - \mathbb{E}[X]) ^2].\]

Other notations: \(Var(X)\), \(\sigma^2(X)\) or \(\sigma^2\) when there is no confusion.

NoteDefinition

Standard deviation.

Standard deviation: \(\sqrt{V[X]}\)

Note, since variance is defined as the expectation of a special function of \(X\), it is possible for variance to not exist (i.e. be infinite).

TipExample

Variance.

Find the variance of the following random variables.

  • The random variable \(X=c\) with probability 1.

  • Bernoulli with parameter \(p\).

  • Uniform on \([a,b]\).

ImportantLemma

Alternative expression of variance.

\[V[X] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2.\]

Proof. Proof. (Hint: Use definition directly.) ◻

TipExample

Variance.

Find the variance of the following random variables using the alternative expression of variance.

  • The random variable \(X=c\) with probability 1.

  • Bernoulli with parameter \(p\).

  • Uniform on \([a,b]\).

Here are some elementary properties of the variance:

  1. \(V[X] \ge 0\) for all \(X\).

  2. \(V[X]\) exists for all bounded random variable \(X\).

  3. \(V[X] =0\) if and only if \(\mathbb{P}(X=c) =1\) for some constant \(c\) (i.e., \(X\) is a constant).

As with expectation, linear transformations of \(X\) have an easier variance formula.

ImportantTheorem

Variance of a linear function of \(X\): \[V[aX+b] = a^2 V[X]\]

Variance of a linear function of \(\mathbf{X}\) where \(\{X_i\}\) are independent: \[V\left[ \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n a_i X_i+b\right] = \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n a^2_i V[X_i]\]

Important special cases of the above theorem under independence are as follows.

  • The variance of the sum of independent random variables. For all \(i=1,...,n\), choose \(a_i=1\) and \(b=0\) to get, \[V\left[ \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i\right] = \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n V[X_i] .\]

  • The variance of the sample mean. For all \(i=1,...,n\), choose \(a_i=1/n\) and \(b=0\) to see that, \[V\left[ \frac{1}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i\right] =\frac{1}{n^2} \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n V[X_i] .\]

  • The variance of the sample mean when \(X_1, \cdots, X_n\) are iid1. For all \(i=1,...,n\), choose \(a_i=1/n\) and \(V[X_i]=\sigma^2\), and \(b=0\), so that: \[V\left[ \frac{1}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^n X_i\right] =\frac{\sigma^2}{n} .\]

TipExample

Find the variance of the \(\text{Binom}(n,p)\) distribution.

TipExample

Suppose \(Z_1,Z_2,...,Z_n\) is a sequence of independent random variables, and \(Z_k \sim N(0,k)\) for \(k=1,...,n\). Find the variance of the sample mean \(\bar Z = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\).
