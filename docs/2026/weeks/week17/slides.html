Lecture 17: Gambler’s ruin

Author

Dr. MY NAME

At the beginning of the class, it was likely mentioned that gambling is a bad idea. With your ample knowledge of probability theory, we can quantify how bad of an idea it is.

ImportantTheorem

Gambling with fair coin.

If two players start with a fixed number of dollars (\(n_A\) and \(n_B\) dollars respectively), and play a gambling game where one dollar is transferred from person A to B probability \(1/2\), and B to A otherwise. The game ends when one person ends up with everything. Then, the probability of person A winning the game is \[\frac{n_A}{n_A + n_B}\] and the probability of person B winning is: \[\frac{n_B}{n_A + n_B}\]

Proof. Proof. (Hint: Call the (random) amount of money person A possesses after \(t\) rounds is \(X_t\). What is \(\mathbb{E}[X_{t+1}]\) in terms of \(\mathbb{E}[X_t]\)? ◻

ImportantTheorem

Gambling with unfair coin.

If two players start with a fixed number of dollars (\(i\) and \(N-i\) dollars respectively), and play a gambling game where one dollar is transferred from person A to B probability \(1/2\), and B to A otherwise. The game ends when one person ends up with everything. Then, the probability of person A winning the game is \[\frac{1-a^{i}}{1-a^{N}},\] where \(a=q/p\), the relative odds of person B winning.

Proof. Proof. (Hint: Call \(P_i\) the probability that person \(A\) will win if they start with \(i\) dollars. How does \(P_i\) relate to \(P_{i+1}\) and \(P_{i-1}\)?) ◻

TipExample

If a person walks into a casino with \(5\) dollars and plays the aforementioned game, how likely it is to double their fortune, when ..

  • The casino is 1.1 times likely to win a given game.

  • The casino is 2 times likely to win a given game.
